Required step: notarize the affidavit

  1. Prepare the application with accurate case information; leave the sworn signature and notary section blank.
  2. Appear before a qualified notary using a method the court accepts. Follow the notary's identification and oath requirements.
  3. Have the notary complete the jurat and applicable seal, then retain the complete notarized document.
  4. Confirm with the clerk or your attorney that the affidavit and service certification are correct before submitting.

An ordinary electronic signature is not notarization. RecordPathAI does not notarize documents. Remote online notarization is not represented as an approved court route unless the clerk confirms acceptance. No payment or automated filing is available through this resource page.

Before filing

  • Confirm the final charge, disposition, applicable waiting period and actual relief with a qualified attorney or official resources.
  • The forms contain a sworn affidavit, including conviction-specific language. For dismissals or acquittals, ask the clerk how to complete it without making inaccurate statements.
  • Confirm notarization, prosecutor service and any certification required by local rules. Leave signature and notary fields for the people who actually sign.
  • Rule 6 restricts filing by nonparties. Rule 8 restricts fax filing where fees apply. Confirm permitted methods, originals, fees, any fee waiver and payment method with the clerk. Do not assume email filing is accepted.
  • Do not send Social Security numbers or confidential client records through ordinary email. Ask the clerk about secure handling and required identifiers.
