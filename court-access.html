The model

A customer service, not a replacement court system.

Customers choose whether to use RecordPathAI's information and packet-preparation assistance. Courts retain control of filing rules, accepted formats, fees, records, staff procedures and judicial decisions.

CUSTOMER EXPERIENCE

What individuals use RecordPathAI for

  • Find the right official court portal and verify case outcomes.
  • Understand potential eligibility, exclusions and waiting-period questions.
  • Identify missing conviction, supervision and final-discharge evidence.
  • Organize and prepare forms for personal review and filing under the court's rules.
COURT EXPERIENCE

What stays with the court

  • Receive filings through existing approved paper or electronic channels.
  • Determine acceptance, fees, schedules and applicable procedures.
  • Make all legal and judicial determinations.
  • Choose whether to consider any future voluntary technology collaboration.

Important: RecordPathAI is not a court, government agency or law firm. It does not represent that any Ohio court has approved, partnered with, endorsed or integrated the service. Proposed 90-day demonstration

Wood County Court of Common Pleas pilot

A voluntary, limited pilot would test whether customer-prepared record-relief packets can be made more complete and easier to process, without accessing or changing Wood County's case-management system. The pilot is a proposal only; it has not been approved by the Court.

01 · RECORD ACCURACY

Verify the actual outcome

Keep initial charges, amended charges, dismissed counts and final convictions separate. Record the source of each disposition rather than assuming an arrest charge became a conviction.

02 · APPLICATION COMPLETENESS

Use both dates the form needs

Capture the conviction or disposition date and the date sentence conditions or supervision actually ended, with separate final-discharge evidence before applying statutory waiting periods.

03 · COURT-APPROVED FILING

Prepare; then use authorized channels

Guide customers through existing Wood County forms and filing instructions. Explore future online submission only if the Court and Clerk authorize it.

04 · OPTIONAL RESEARCH

Officer identification and transparency

Separately examine whether arresting-officer names, roles and agencies can be accurately supported by lawfully accessible records, without adding work to the sealing application process.

Why the date distinction matters: The Wood County Criminal Extract Report field labeled “Termination Date” is drawn from the most recently inserted case-disposition record. It does not, by itself, establish that community control, parole, post-release control or all other sentence conditions ended. Actual sentence termination and final discharge must be independently verified. A missing value must be flagged—not guessed.

Operational & fiscal opportunity

Less preventable rework. More accessible, lawful filings.

Better-prepared customer submissions may lower staff time spent clarifying incomplete packets. If more genuinely eligible residents complete the filing process, total lawful fee collections could also rise. These outcomes are hypotheses to test, not promised savings or revenue.

Measure administrative efficiency

  • Percentage of packets complete on first review.
  • Missing-document and correction-request rates.
  • Staff minutes spent on intake clarification.
  • Filing rejection or return rates, with reasons.

Measure actual access and fee activity

  • Customer conversion from guidance to completed application.
  • Number of submitted, accepted and fee-waived applications.
  • Gross filing fees actually collected.
  • Statutory state and county/municipal distributions, if documented.

Low-burden test plan

Proposed 90-day pilot phases

DAYS 01–30

Requirements and synthetic demonstration

Document court forms and current submission practices; use fictional case examples. Agree on evaluation measures and approval requirements.

DAYS 31–60

Voluntary limited evaluation

Subject to approvals and customer consent, test completed packet quality and source verification. Continue existing court filing channels.

DAYS 61–90

Report and recommendations

Compare completeness and time measures; document blockers, privacy lessons and whether a larger pilot is warranted.

No required API, clerk dashboard, case-management connection, automated judicial eligibility decision or operational change is assumed. Any real-case data processing, staff participation or electronic-filing integration would require separate authorization.

Optional, separate workstream

Verified officer identification for public accountability

The Clerk's historical extracts do not provide a reliable structured arresting-officer field. A separate feasibility study could evaluate what public records, if any, lawfully establish an officer's name, agency and actual role in a specific case. The Court would not be asked to create police records it does not maintain.

  • Source first: distinguish arresting, investigating, reporting and charging personnel; preserve document provenance and verification status.
  • Fairness: distinguish allegations, charges and findings; never portray an officer's involvement as proof of misconduct.
  • Privacy and restrictions: exclude sealed, expunged, confidential or otherwise restricted case information and avoid public release of unverified identifications.
  • Separate operations: keep this research optional and independent of an individual's ability to prepare and submit an application for record relief.
